## What was asked

The design counts a worker only if a birth year and sex can be read from the 2023, 2021 or 2019
individual registers. A worker in none of the three, in practice one first registered in Sweden in
2024 or 2025, is not counted. That is the editor's own mechanism in its purest form: if those
workers sit disproportionately in exposed firms and at young ages, part of our fall is measurement.
Until now the paper could say such workers exist and could not say how many.

## What the count shows

The employer declaration carries no birth year and no age of its own, so a worker with no register
row cannot be placed in an age band and the share is reported unbroken by age. The script probed
the catalogue rather than assuming this.

Share of employer-declaration person-months whose worker reaches none of the three registers:

| year | all | Q4, the treated group | Q1 to Q3 | gap, percentage points |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2019 | 0.612 | 0.567 | 0.620 | -0.053 |
| 2020 | 0.205 | 0.273 | 0.183 | +0.090 |
| 2021 | 0.187 | 0.228 | 0.175 | +0.053 |
| 2022 | 0.188 | 0.227 | 0.175 | +0.051 |
| 2023 | 0.204 | 0.279 | 0.184 | +0.094 |
| 2024 | 0.195 | 0.252 | 0.184 | +0.068 |
| 2025 | 0.210 | 0.269 | 0.196 | +0.073 |

Two readings, and the second is the one that answers the editor.

**The share does not rise after the register ends.** It averages 0.279 per cent over 2019 to 2023
and 0.203 over 2024 and 2025. A register that stops in 2023 does not leave a growing pool of
uncounted workers, because birth year and sex, unlike occupation, can be read from any of three
vintages and almost every worker appears in one.

**The exposure gap is real but flat.** Uncounted payslips are more common in the top exposure
quartile than in the lower three, by 0.065 percentage points on average over 2020 to 2022 and by
0.071 over 2024 and 2025. What our design differences out is the level; what could manufacture the
step is a change in the gap, and that change is **0.006 percentage points**. Workers aged 22 to 25
are 4.6 per cent of employment in the top quartile, so even if every one of those additional
uncounted payslips belonged to a young worker, the implied fall in measured young employment is
0.13 per cent, against an estimated step of 2.6 per cent: one part in twenty.

Also exported, and useful for the sample description: the share of person-months sitting in an
employer the 2019 score book scored falls from 99.1 per cent in 2019 to 92.5 per cent in 2025, as
employers founded after 2019 take on staff.

## Where it goes

Online Appendix Part VI, beside the sentence that currently says such workers are not counted, and
one sentence in the response to the editor's coverage objection, which this bounds directly. It is
a count and not an estimate: it bounds a measurement channel and does not test it.
