**Why.** Script 73 reads the employer's three-digit industry from LISA's firm table for 2019
alone (`Ftg_2019`, `Org_Sni2007`). That table holds 552,099 coded firms and it is the right
source, but 5,285 of the 111,459 employers on the 22-25 panel and 9,106 of the 128,193 on the
26-30 panel are not in it. Scripts 73, 78 and 79 then gave each unmatched firm a cluster of its
own, which is why lane 25's Part C reports **5,545 clusters at 22-25 and 9,368 at 26-30 against
only 265 real three-digit groups**: nineteen clusters in twenty are one firm, and what Table 1
now calls industry clustering is a hybrid of industry and employer clustering. The same key
also decides which firms enter the industry-by-age-by-month test of 78's Part F and 79's Part B.
The likely mechanism is a reference-date mismatch and not a bad join: `Ftg` is LISA's firm table
and LISA is built on a November reference, while our panel comes from the monthly employer
declarations, so a firm that employed somebody earlier in 2019 but nobody in November is in our
panel and absent from `Ftg_2019`. Part A measures that rather than asserting it.

Parts are chosen with `CANARIES_80_PARTS` (default ABC) and the folder with `CANARIES_80_OUT`.

**Read rules, fixed before the runs and printed by the script at the start and in the summary:**

- **A.** Descriptive. Coverage is reported at every step and the employers `Ftg_2019` missed are
  described beside the ones it found, whichever way the comparison falls. If they are smaller and

- **B. GATE:** the coefficients must reproduce the employer-clustered run to four decimals.

- **C.** No gate. The retained share is reported whatever it is, and so is the share of firms in

  carried-forward code is noisier, noise in the absorbing dimension makes it absorb less, and a
  retained share that rose for that reason would flatter us.

**What to expect, so it is not read as a bug.** (1) The cluster count falls from about 5,500 to
about 266, which is the point; 266 groups is comfortably above the range in which a
cluster-robust variance stops being trustworthy. (2) The industry standard errors should RISE,
because the earlier ones were part employer-clustered, and that bears first on the 22-25 step,
which lane 25 already could not distinguish from zero. **Table 1's industry column and the
industry sentences in Results and OA III.2 are provisional until this lands.** (3) Part A may
report slightly fewer than 552,099 firms at the `Ftg_2019` step: script 73 strips the non-digits
out of a code, so an unclassified `00000` survives it as the group `000`, which SNI 2007 does not
have. The summary's conventions counts say which and how many.
