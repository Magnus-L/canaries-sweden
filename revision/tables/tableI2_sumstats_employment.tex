\begin{minipage}{0.95\textwidth}\footnotesize\vspace{4pt}The panel is balanced over employers, age bands and months and zero-filled, so \textbf{a firm whose young headcount falls to zero contributes those months}: the extensive margin is in the estimate, which is why Poisson rather than a log transform. ``Zero throughout'' counts employer-band cells with no employment in any month of the window; these are perfectly predicted by the employer-by-age effect and fixest separates them regardless, so they are removed before estimation at no cost to any coefficient. The first three columns are counted before the exposure merge and so are the same whatever the score. ``Skeleton'' is what remains, and ``Estimated'' the cells surviving the merge with the employer's 2019 occupation mix. A firm enters only if it holds the young band and at least one older band at some point from January 2021, which is a pre-treatment property and not an outcome. Exposure is scored for 262{,}089 firms. The paper reports the flow margins at 22--25 only, so the two 26--30 flow rows carry a skeleton and no estimate; nothing is carried across from another score. Panel~B is why the employer counts differ across the paper's exhibits. A within-employer age comparison can only be identified by an employer that holds both of the ages being compared, so each comparison has its own panel. The headline panels are anchored on the young band, the profile panels on the reference band 41--49; the profile panels therefore include employers with no worker aged 22--25 at all, whose cells are zero throughout and which contribute nothing to that band's coefficient. A count printed beside a profile estimate is the panel's and not the number of employers identifying that coefficient, and the two are not comparable. The widest row imposes no age requirement at all.
