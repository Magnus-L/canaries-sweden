# Same Storm, Different Boats: Generative AI and Young Workers Within Firms

Replication package for Lodefalk, Löthman, Koch and Engberg (2026), "Same Storm,
Different Boats: Generative AI and Young Workers Within Firms", under revision at
*Economics Letters*. Örebro University Working Paper 2026:2 and Ratio Working Paper 388
are the submitted version.

**What the paper asks.** After 2022 stock prices rose and job postings fell, and
generative AI and monetary tightening are rival explanations. Sweden separates the
two in time: the Riksbank's first rate rise came in April 2022, seven months before
the ChatGPT launch. The paper reads both dates on two margins. On the posting margin,
4.9 million Platsbanken advertisements matched to the DAIOE generative-AI exposure
index show postings falling in every exposure quartile from the rate rise, with no
statistically significant additional decline in exposed occupations after the launch.
On the employment margin, monthly employer declarations covering every employee in
Sweden show that inside the same employer the employment of workers aged 22 to 25
relative to their older colleagues rose during the tightening months and fell from
2024, when Statistics Sweden records firm AI use rising from 10 to 25 per cent. The
relative change is concentrated in the advantage of colleagues aged 50 to 69, runs
through separations rather than hiring, and falls on young women, largely within the
same education tracks as young men.

**What this repository holds.** The code behind every number in the paper, its
online appendix and the response to the editor, the aggregate exports that leave
Statistics Sweden's secure environment, and the scripts that turn those exports into
the paper's tables and figures. Every register script's docstring says, in this
order, what question it answers, what it estimates, what it reads and writes, and
where its numbers appear.

## Why the design changed

The submitted version compared young workers in AI-exposed occupations with young
workers in less exposed occupations inside the same employer. That needs a current
occupation code on every young worker in every month, and Sweden's occupation
register is published with a two-year lag. An as-of backtest (`revision/mona/45`),
which imposes the staleness of the 2024 and 2025 records on years where the truth is
observable, showed that the lag alone moves the coefficient from +0.019 to -0.288,
more than the whole of the -0.174 the submitted version reported. The design was
withdrawn rather than defended.

The design the paper now reports scores each employer once, in 2019, from the
education mix of its incumbents aged 31 to 69, gives each education group the
employment-weighted mean generative-AI exposure of the occupations its holders held
that year, and compares each young band with the older bands inside the same employer
and month. No worker under 31 enters the exposure measure, no occupation code recorded
after 2019 enters anything, and the outcome is read from the employer declarations and
the birth year alone. The specification is Equation (2) of the paper: Poisson
pseudo-maximum likelihood on employer by age band by month counts, with
employer-by-month, employer-by-age and month-by-age effects, a Riksbank interaction
from April 2022, an interim term from the launch, an adoption step from January 2024,
three calendar-quarter interactions with the fourth quarter omitted, and standard
errors clustered by employer.

## Reproducing the posting margin (public data)

Software: Python 3.10 or later with the packages in `requirements.txt`, plus
`pyfixest` for the Poisson posting regressions in `revision/local/l05`, `l06`, `l08`
and `l09`. About 8 GB of memory for streaming the JSONL archives; about 6 GB of disk
for the raw archives. Stata 18 was used for an independent replication of the
submitted posting regression (`src/replication_stata.do`) and of the submitted
employer design (`src/mona_canaries_regression.do`).

1. The submitted pipeline: `python src/run_all.py` downloads the 2020 to 2025
   archives, processes them to occupation-by-month aggregates, fetches the DAIOE index,
   the stock indices and the policy rate, and produces the posting regressions, figures
   and tables of the submitted version (steps 01 to 13; `--skip-download` and
   `--from-step` are available). The processed files it writes under `data/processed/`
   are read by the revision's scripts. Of its outputs, the current manuscript still uses
   `did_regression.tex`, `robustness_results.tex`, `eloundou_robustness.tex`,
   `top_bottom_occupations.tex`, the Sweden-US, OMXSPI, quartile, event-study,
   rate-sensitivity, teleworkability, Rambachan-Roth and policy-rate figures of OA Part
   II, and `omxs30_monthly.csv` for the upper panel of Figure 1.
2. The revision's local chain, in this order: `revision/local/l01` (accounting and
   coverage by source; about twenty minutes over the raw archives), `l02` (coverage on
   the processed data), `l08` (the 2026 quarters, the extended panel and Equation (1)
   on both windows), `l03`, `l05`, `l06` (the decile gradient, the Poisson variants and
   the seasonality variants on the extended panel), `l04` (the published aggregates),
   `l09` and `l09 --variants` (the within-employer posting design), `l09b` (its
   heterogeneity), then `l07` (Figure 1 and the posting event study) and `l10` (the
   posting, firm-lane and public-data tables, written into the paper repository).
   `revision/run_local.py` runs l01 to l07 in sequence; `revision/config.py` holds the
   paths and the analysis constants (the two treatment dates, the descriptive cut at
   December 2025 and the regression window to June 2026).

Two inputs of this chain are not in the repository. `l08` reads the 2026 quarter files
from the AIEL Monitor's cached corpus (`~/.cache/aiel-jobads/`), which are the
JobTech closed-quarter files under their own names. `l09` and `l09b` read the
Monitor's employer-by-month-by-occupation cube (`firm_month_v2.csv.gz`, built from
the same public corpus by the Monitor's pipeline with the employer's organisation
number read from each advertisement) and the SCB business register bulk; both are
public data but the cube is a derived file that lives in the lab's infrastructure, and
its population and counting unit are stated in `tables/firm_within_meta.txt`.

## Reproducing the register results (MONA)

The scripts in `revision/mona/` run inside Statistics Sweden's MONA environment on
the P1207 share, where the project folder holds an `input/` folder (the DAIOE
quartiles, the Eloundou and teleworkability scores and the education key as `.dta`
files, verified by hash before any SQL runs), the scripts, a `cache/` folder for the
expensive pulls, and one `output_NN/` folder per script. Every script is
self-contained: it is submitted as one file to SCB's batch client, opens its own log
(`output_NN/NN_log.txt`, with a provenance header), reads the caches it needs, fits
through R (`fixest` 0.13.2 on R 4.5.0, located automatically or by a pin file), and
writes its aggregates and a summary to `output_NN/`. A batch job can take no
arguments, so options travel as environment variables set inside a wrapper.

**Dependency order.** `47h` pulls the education weights and the 2019 to 2023 year
frames and builds the score books; `47j` defines the employer exposure and its fixed
effects; `47L` pulls the 2019 occupation baseline and the monthly counts by employer
and age band to June 2025; `54` pulls the hires and separations; `61` builds the
panel of Equation (2) from those caches and defines the adoption dating. From there
`63`, `65`, `66` and `67` (which pulls the sex panel) read the caches; `68` produces
the headline; `70` builds the six-band skeleton for `74`; `71`, `72`, `73`, `75`,
`76` (which pulls the sex-by-education panel) and `77` complete the battery. The
submitted design's panel comes from `39` (writes `cache/panel_vintage.parquet`) and
serves `40`, `41` and `46`; `45` pulls its own dual-coded panels; `49` runs its own
person-level pull.

**Runners.** `_lane.py` with the `run_lane*.py` wrappers runs a list of scripts in
one batch job, skips a stage whose summary file already exists, and keeps its own
capped log; `run_all_mona.py` is the runner for the submitted design's battery
(`39` to `46`) and carries `--retire-caches` for the end of a round. Caches are
parquet files written atomically and validated against the columns each caller
needs, so a cache written before a change to a pull is rebuilt rather than reused.

**Exports.** Only aggregates leave MONA, through Statistics Sweden's disclosure
control, with counts below five suppressed by `mona_common.enforce_min_cell`. Each
export lands in `revision/output/<round and stage>/` under the file names the scripts
write; the table and figure scripts in `revision/local/` read the export folders the
manifest names and take one other folder on the command line.

**Testing outside MONA.** `revision/mona/test_dryrun.py` compiles the upload set,
imports every script with `CANARIES_DRYRUN=1` (no database driver, SQL raises), and
runs the panel builders and the three R wrappers on synthetic data; the
`revision/local/test_*.py` files do the same per script. `revision/stage_upload.py
--check` verifies that `revision/upload/` (the staged copies with `MANIFEST.txt`)
matches `revision/mona/`.

## What can and cannot be reproduced without register access

Without MONA access a reader can reproduce, from the repository and public data,
Figure 1, every posting regression of Section 3 and Online Appendix Part II, the
within-employer posting design of Part V (given the Monitor cube described above),
the published-aggregates check of III.5, and every register table and figure of the
current draft from the exported aggregates in `revision/output/`: Table 1 (`l18`),
Figures 2, 3, A1 and A2 (`l23`, `l14`, `l15`, `l16`), OA Tables III.1, III.2 and IV.1
to IV.3 (`l24`, `l19`, `l25`, `l21`). OA Table I.2 (`l22`) additionally parses
`68_log.txt`, which the repository's ignore rule for log files keeps out of the public
tree.

What cannot be reproduced without access is the path from the registers to those
exports: every coefficient in Section 2's employment paragraph, Section 3, Table 1
and Online Appendix Parts I.2, III and IV is estimated on individual-level records
that never leave Statistics Sweden. The code that produces them is here in full, with
the hashes of the copies that ran recorded in the manifest and in
`revision/upload/MANIFEST.txt`, so a reader with P1207 access, or with equivalent
registers, can run it as it ran.
